## Acknowledgments

This book began as informal conversations with colleagues at Northeastern University about whether and how to deploy NotebookLM in their classrooms. The first sketch of the chapter outline emerged from those conversations, and the chapters that followed are better for the working teachers — at every level, from elementary specialist to research faculty — who read drafts, pushed back on overclaims, and corrected the cases where my framing was wrong. Particular thanks to the Humanitarians AI fellows whose questions made several chapters of this book necessary, and to the OPT-fellow research collaborators who tested the privacy-and-equity framing of Chapter 11 against deployments I would not have thought to consider. The Google research-partnership announcements in May 2026 — Purdue, University of Alabama, UC Riverside — give me hope that the evidence base will, by the time this book is in its second edition, be thicker than I had to describe it in the first.

Errors and overclaims that remain are mine.

## About the Author

**Nik Bear Brown** is an Associate Teaching Professor in the College of Engineering at Northeastern University, where he teaches AI, data science, programming, and the design of AI-assisted education infrastructure. He founded *Humanitarians AI* (a 501(c)(3) supporting graduate students building consequential AI projects) and *Bear Brown & Company*, which publishes the *Practitioner Guides for the AI Classroom* series this book belongs to. His current work centers on the *Irreducibly Human* framework — a curriculum project on the cognitive capacities the AI era most urgently requires humans to develop — and on *Medhavy*, an AI-native adaptive tutoring platform that this book is designed to integrate with.

He holds a PhD in Computer Science from UCLA (computational and systems biology, AI, statistics), an MS in Information Design and Data Visualization from Northeastern, an MBA from Northeastern, and a BA in Biochemistry and Molecular Biology from UC Santa Cruz. He did a part-time postdoc in deep learning at Harvard Medical School while teaching at Northeastern. Earlier in his career he worked as a molecular biologist at DNAX Research Institute and Cetus Corporation.

He writes at [nikbearbrown.com](https://www.nikbearbrown.com), at [irreducibly.xyz](https://irreducibly.xyz), and at [skepticism.ai](https://www.skepticism.ai). He works in Boston.

The connective thread across his work is simple: AI is powerful, but power without judgment is just a microscope sitting in a box. This book is one attempt to put the right humans in the room with the microscope, looking at the right thing, asking the right questions.

## Why no Index

This book is published primarily as a Kindle edition and as an online resource integrated with **[Medhavy](https://www.medhavy.com/)** — *Medhavy AI*, the AI-native adaptive tutoring and intelligent-textbook platform whose name comes from the Sanskrit मेधावी (*medhavy*), meaning "intelligent" or "intellectually brilliant."

Kindle readers do not need an index. The Kindle app's full-text search retrieves every occurrence of any term in seconds — better than a hand-built index could. Online readers of the Medhavy-integrated version get the same affordance and more: the platform indexes the book against your course's source material, surfaces the chapter sections relevant to any specific deployment question you are working through, and lets you ask the book in the same way you would ask a tutor.

If a print edition is produced for institutional use, an index will be compiled professionally for that release. For the current Kindle and online editions, full-text search is the index. *Come learn something with us.*

## Glossary

Selected key terms from the book, in plain language. Chapter where the term is introduced is noted in parentheses.

- **Active engagement (assignment)** — Assignment designed so the AI shortcut produces a *worse* artifact than the engaged path. (Ch 5)
- **Audit trail** — The citation link in a NotebookLM output that lets the reader verify against the source. Not a guarantee; an opportunity. (Ch 1)
- **Backward design** — Wiggins & McTighe's framework: start from the learning goal, work back to assessment evidence, then design instruction. (Ch 7)
- **Bounded tool** — An AI tool that answers only from user-provided sources, with citations. NotebookLM is the canonical educational example. (Ch 1)
- **Citation discipline** — The practice of treating every AI-output citation as something to be clicked and verified, not trusted on appearance. (Ch 1–2)
- **Deep Research mode** — NotebookLM feature (Nov 2025+) that lets the model search the web for material not in the uploaded sources. Off by default. (Ch 1, Ch 9)
- **Education Plus** — Google's premium NotebookLM tier for qualifying institutions; higher quotas than the free tier. (Ch 11)
- **Fabrication vs. substitution** — Two distinct integrity problems. Source-grounding addresses fabrication; assignment design addresses substitution. (Ch 8)
- **FERPA / COPPA** — U.S. student-data privacy laws. NotebookLM under Workspace for Education is FERPA/COPPA-compliant; under personal accounts it is not. (Ch 11)
- **Four-bucket framework** — Institutional exemplars / conference papers / emerging case studies / learning-science plausibility. The honest-evidence taxonomy. (Ch 14)
- **Four-question framework** — Goal / corpus / citation / privacy. The tool-selection decision tree. (Ch 12)
- **Frictional principle** — The biological argument that cognitive struggle triggers the neurochemistry that produces durable learning. Bypass the struggle and you bypass the learning. (Appendix A)
- **Hallucination** — Fluent AI output unsupported by sources or training data. Source-grounding reduces (does not eliminate) the rate. (Ch 1)
- **Interactive Mode** — Audio Overview configuration that lets the listener pause the AI hosts and ask a clarifying question, converting consumption into dialogue. (Ch 3, Ch 5)
- **LearnLM** — The Gemini variant fine-tuned for learning-science principles that powers NotebookLM in education. (Ch 1)
- **Learning Guide** — NotebookLM feature that delivers diagnostic questions. Configurable for self-testing (Monash model). (Ch 10)
- **Note-to-Source loop** — Pin a chat response as a Note, promote it to a source, generate against teacher-framed material. Captures pedagogical content knowledge. (Ch 3, Ch 4, Ch 9)
- **Passive substitution** — Assignment where the AI shortcut produces the same artifact as the engaged path. The failure mode. (Ch 5)
- **Phase gate** — The explicit decision about where AI handling stops and human work begins. The book's load-bearing concept. (Introduction, Appendix A)
- **Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)** — The architectural pattern: retrieve relevant passages, then generate from them. NotebookLM is RAG tuned for education. (Ch 1)
- **Silent ingestion failure** — A source that appears in the Sources panel but whose content the model cannot actually use. (Ch 2)
- **Source-grounded** — AI that answers only from user-provided documents, with citations. The opposite of open-loop. (Ch 1)
- **Three-question assessment audit** — Goal? Substitution possible? Substitution is the goal? Run before every assessment. (Ch 7)
- **Tier 1 / Tiers 4–7** — Cognitive-capacity layers in the Irreducibly Human framework. AI is strong at Tier 1, structurally weak at Tiers 4–7. (Appendix A)
- **Verification stack** — The three-step check on every output: passage exists, supports the claim, omits nothing critical. (Ch 2)
- **Workspace for Education** — Google's institutional account type with FERPA/COPPA/GDPR compliance and training-data exclusion. (Ch 11)
