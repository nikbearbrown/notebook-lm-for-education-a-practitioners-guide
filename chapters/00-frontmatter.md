## Preface

I started writing this book the third time a colleague asked me the same question.

The question had a different surface each time. *Should I use NotebookLM with my freshmen, or is it going to make them stop reading?* *My department head wants every faculty member to deploy AI tools by next semester — where do I even begin?* *I tried Audio Overviews for one unit and the quiz scores went down. What did I do wrong?* The questions were operationally different. They were the same question underneath: *how do I tell whether this tool is actually helping?*

The honest answer is that, as of mid-2026, the published evidence base on NotebookLM in education is thin relative to the enthusiasm. The strongest single study covers 102 undergraduate students in five communication and media courses at one university in Bahrain (Mohamed, 2025). It is suggestive. It is not definitive. Most of what we have are institutional exemplars — Monash's self-testing model, the University of Wisconsin–Milwaukee's Math 94 audio companion, the UIC research workflow — plus a small set of conference papers and the underlying learning-science findings the tool's features map onto.

The thinness of the evidence is precisely why this book exists. Educators who wait for definitive outcome data before deploying any AI tool will be waiting in 2030. Educators who deploy without a framework for honest evaluation will produce the cycle of enthusiasm-followed-by-disappointment that has marked most edtech adoption since the smart-board era. There is a third path: deploy carefully, design around what the tool actually does well, and develop the institutional language to describe what you are seeing — including what you are not seeing. That is the book this book tries to be.

I wrote it because I teach in a department that has deployed every variety of AI tool with every variety of result, because I have spent the last two years building infrastructure for AI-in-education at Northeastern, and because I have watched too many talented teachers reach for AI tools with no framework for distinguishing what works from what only looks like it working. The book is the framework I wish I had handed them the first time.

I did not write this book to argue that NotebookLM will transform education. I did not write it to argue that NotebookLM is a distraction from real teaching. I wrote it because the actual answer — *NotebookLM is a useful bounded tool with a thin evidence base, strong adoption evidence, and real deployment risks that can be managed with deliberate design* — is harder to say in a sentence than either of those positions. Hard-to-say-in-a-sentence is exactly what a book is for.

What this book does not cover: it does not survey every AI tool that has touched education since 2022. It is not a feature reference for NotebookLM — Google's documentation does that better and updates faster. It is not a treatise on the future of artificial intelligence. It is a practitioner's handbook for educators who have already decided NotebookLM is worth thirty minutes of their attention and want to know what to do with that thirty minutes.

The rest of the book is the answer to that question.

— Nik Bear Brown, Boston, May 2026
